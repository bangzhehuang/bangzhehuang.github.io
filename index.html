Selected Research

  1. AI4AI: Learning to Design a Model

    33 SOTA-level results across 9 OpenTS datasets. I designed a neural architecture search space rich in high-performing models, enabling straightforward random search and performance-guided mutation to discover strong architectures. The resulting models matched or exceeded published SOTA scores on 33 forecasting tasks, with up to 31.56% lower MSE. I also built the multi-GPU search and evaluation infrastructure that turns architecture design into a scalable experimental process.

    Lead researcher · Advisor: Prof. Ziming Liu, College of AI, Tsinghua University · Ongoing research

    Demo · Code

  2. Scaling Foundation Models for Many-Body Physics

    I built and trained a Hamiltonian-conditioned foundation model for condensed-matter physics, spanning transverse-field Ising, Heisenberg, XXZ/XYZ, and Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interactions. The project combines mixture-of-architectures research with quantum wavefunction learning across thousands of Hamiltonians. I led the full training stack, including physical-system representation, multi-GPU JAX training, mixed precision, checkpoint recovery, live monitoring, and physics-based evaluation.

    Project lead · Advisor: Prof. Jing Wang, Department of Physics, Fudan University · Preprint in preparation

  3. Open-Ended Scientific Research Benchmark for Physics Research Agents

    How can open-ended physics research agents be evaluated without prescribing a single valid trajectory? I designed benchmark tasks whose ground truth is a low-dimensional physical structure and equivalent observables, while agents may reach it through multi-round simulation, observation, and feedback. A pilot tests the full loop from experimental design and code execution to a final physical audit.

    Independent research · Academic discussion: Prof. Siheng Chen, School of Artificial Intelligence, Shanghai Jiao Tong University · Pilot completed

  4. Phase Transport and Defect Dynamics in Nonreciprocal Spin Systems

    I study the microscopic mechanism behind macroscopic oscillation in two-dimensional nonreciprocal spin systems. Using Monte Carlo simulations, I connect oscillation frequency, domain-wall density, and effective phase-transport velocity through ω = ρveff. The analysis identifies two transport regimes: a small-J regime dominated by vortex-pair generation and domain-wall expansion, and a large-J regime governed by vortex-constrained topological structures, with robustness across lattices and bond disorder.

    Lead researcher · Advisor: Prof. Zi Cai, School of Physics and Astronomy, Shanghai Jiao Tong University · Pilot completed
